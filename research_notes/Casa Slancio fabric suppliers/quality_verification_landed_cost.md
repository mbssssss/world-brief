# Casa Slancio: Verifying Fabric Suppliers and Fabric Quality, and Estimating Landed Cost (China/India to UAE)

Scope: A small UAE premium apparel brand buying performance knit (120–160 gsm) and stretch-woven (110–145 gsm) fabric for 300–1,000 garments per style, sewn in Ajman. Research date: 25 Nov 2026. Currency notes: AED is pegged at about 3.6725 per USD. INR conversions use about 85–90 INR/USD (an approximate 2025–26 range I did not verify in this session). All "Inferences" are my reasoning, not sourced facts.

## Q1. Supplier verification: telling a real mill from a trader, what the badges mean, Trade Assurance, and payment norms

### Takeaway
Alibaba badges show that a company exists and has paid for verification. They do not show that it is the mill. The strongest free test is the business scope on the Chinese business licence, checked on GSXT: look for manufacturing verbs (生产/制造/加工) rather than only trading verbs (销售/批发/贸易). Back this up with the label on the SGS on-site video and a live video walk of the factory floor. Trade Assurance covers only what is written into the online order, and you must claim within 30 days of receipt. So the order must spell out measurable fabric specs, and you should tie the balance payment to a passed inspection or test.

### Cited Findings
**What the badges mean**
- "Gold Supplier" is a paid membership tier. Any supplier willing to pay the annual fee can show it. "Verified Supplier" requires an on-site inspection by a third-party auditor such as TÜV Rheinland or SGS. Buyers should still do their own due diligence on top of it. — [Aqualora: 7 red flags](https://www.aqualora.us/blog/vet-alibaba-suppliers-red-flags)
- Alibaba's A&V (Authentication & Verification) checks confirm that a company exists and is registered. They do not confirm that it manufactures rather than trades, because Alibaba accepts both types and lets the supplier self-declare. An audited "Verified Manufacturer" badge is a stronger signal but is still only one data point. — [ChineseCheck: 12 methods](https://chinesecheck.com/blog/identify-real-factory-vs-trading-company)
- The "Custom Manufacturer" tag is also open to "Industrial & Trading Co., Ltd" (工贸一体) hybrids. "Multispecialty suppliers" are almost always trading companies. "Verified" Gold Suppliers have had their company and factory physically checked by Alibaba's SGS team. — [KOI Sourcing](https://koisourcing.com/blog/how-to-avoid-fake-alibaba-suppliers)
- A sourcing veteran says neither the Verified Supplier badge nor the business licence tells you whether a supplier is a factory or a trader. This is a forum post, not an authoritative source. — [Reddit r/Alibaba](https://www.reddit.com/r/Alibaba/comments/1tc69fh/how_to_tell_if_your_alibaba_supplier_is_actually/)

**Checks that tell a trader from a mill**
- **Business licence scope (经营范围):** look up the Unified Social Credit Code on China's National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn), which is free and official. A factory's scope includes 生产 (produce), 制造 (manufacture) or 加工 (process) plus the product category. A pure trader's scope is limited to 销售 (sales), 批发 (wholesale) or 贸易 (trade). A business licence is a public document and must be displayed at the premises, so refusing to share it is a significant red flag. — [ChineseCheck](https://chinesecheck.com/blog/identify-real-factory-vs-trading-company)
- **Company name and address:** a Chinese name ending in 贸易有限责任公司 ("Trading Co., Ltd") usually means a trader. An address in a "Creative Park" (创意园) or a commercial office tower usually means a sales office. Check the registered address on Baidu Maps; real factories sit in industrial zones. — [KOI Sourcing](https://koisourcing.com/blog/how-to-avoid-fake-alibaba-suppliers)
- **SGS on-site inspection video label:** "Supplier's affiliate / partner / associate company" is a red flag for a trader. "Supplier's company" or no relationship label is a green flag. Also check that the Chinese name on the factory gate matches the licence. — [KOI Sourcing](https://koisourcing.com/blog/how-to-avoid-fake-alibaba-suppliers)
- **Product range:** a showroom that spans unrelated categories points to a trader or sourcing agency. — [ChineseCheck](https://chinesecheck.com/blog/identify-real-factory-vs-trading-company)
- **Live video tour:** a 20–30 minute live WeChat or Zoom walk of the floor costs nothing. Traders refuse, send pre-recorded clips, or show irrelevant facilities. In one case cited, a trader was adding about 35% markup over the real manufacturer. — [Aqualora](https://www.aqualora.us/blog/vet-alibaba-suppliers-red-flags)
- **Test reports:** they should be recent (within 12–18 months), issued to the specific factory, and cover your exact specification. Verify them with the issuing lab. — [Aqualora](https://www.aqualora.us/blog/vet-alibaba-suppliers-red-flags)
- **How common traders are:** industry estimates from forums and agents put the share of self-declared "manufacturers" on Alibaba or Made-in-China that are really traders at 30–50% ([ChineseCheck](https://chinesecheck.com/blog/identify-real-factory-vs-trading-company)). Another blog claims about 60% of Alibaba listings are traders ([Aqualora](https://www.aqualora.us/blog/vet-alibaba-suppliers-red-flags)). Both figures are unsourced estimates and they conflict, so treat them as order-of-magnitude only.

**Alibaba Trade Assurance**
- It covers payments made through Alibaba.com when the supplier ships late, does not ship, or ships goods that miss the quality written into the online order. If Alibaba's dispute team rules for the buyer, Alibaba can refund on the supplier's behalf, up to the amount paid into the order. The only cost is the payment-method processing fee. — [Importivity](https://importivity.com/blog/alibaba-trade-assurance/)
- **Claim window:** within 30 days of confirmed receipt, or 60 days for Enterprise and Enterprise Pro buyers. — [Alibaba money-back policy page](https://tradeassurance.alibaba.com/ta/moneybackpolicy.htm); [Importivity](https://importivity.com/blog/alibaba-trade-assurance/)
- **Exclusions:** no advance refund when the claim goes beyond Trade Assurance's scope, the supplier is found not liable, the buyer's information is false or cannot be checked, or the buyer does not help with a return the ruling requires. — [Importivity](https://importivity.com/blog/alibaba-trade-assurance/)
- **Quality claims:** Alibaba's own seller guide says subjective complaints "without objective standards" are not protected. Evidence means a third-party inspection report, photos or video, and samples. It also cites a 15-day inspection window and 7–10 business-day refunds. — [Alibaba seller blog 2026](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/b2b-trade/trade-assurance-complete-guide-alibaba-secure-payment)
- **Conflicting windows:** one secondary source says disputes must be filed "usually 15 to 30 days" after delivery ([Dapsypay](https://dapsypay.com/blogs/alibaba-trade-assurance-explained-what-it-covers-and-how-refunds-work-in-2026)). Alibaba's own policy page says 30 or 60 days.

**Payment terms**
- The common Chinese supplier term is "30% T/T deposit, 70% balance against B/L copy before shipment". The source recommends a 30/40/30 split for new relationships. A 25/75 split is favourable to the buyer but rare. — [ChineseCheck: T/T guide](https://chinesecheck.com/blog/tt-payment-chinese-supplier)
- For a first order, aim for a small deposit with the balance tied to inspection, for example 30% upfront and 70% after a passed pre-shipment inspection. — [Novex Global](https://www.novexglobal.com/safe-payment-terms-importing-overseas-suppliers/)
- One QC firm warns buyers not to accept a supplier's claim that 30/70 is "the one standard". — [QualityInspection.org](https://qualityinspection.org/payment-terms-chinese-manufacturer/)

### Inferences
- In fabric, traders and converters are not automatically bad. At 300–1,000 garments per style (roughly 250–900 m per fabric), many knit mills' per-colour MOQs will be too high, and converters or stock-service traders may be the realistic source. The aim is to know which one you are dealing with, and to require the mill name plus a mill-issued or lab-issued test report for the exact article (and ideally the lot).
- Put measurable specs in the Trade Assurance order so a quality claim has an objective standard: composition, GSM ±5%, cuttable width, ISO 6330 shrinkage ≤3%, ISO 12945-2 pilling ≥4, ISO 105-C06 ≥4, and so on. Attach the approved lab dip or strike-off reference.
- The 30-day claim window is short compared with a supply chain of sea freight → Ajman cut → wash testing. To keep any recourse, test on receipt, or better, test pre-shipment.
- Letters of credit (LC): I found no source on LC costs for small importers. Practitioners generally regard LCs as uneconomic below roughly USD 10–20k because of bank fees and document rigidity (unverified).

### Gaps
- IndiaMART verification (for example "TrustSEAL" and "Verified Exporter" badges) and Made-in-China.com "Audited Supplier" were not researched in this session. A natural parallel to the GSXT check for India would be verifying the supplier's GSTIN and IEC (Importer-Exporter Code) on Indian government portals. This is my inference and was not verified here.
- No source found for LC fees or Alibaba escrow fee percentages in 2026.
- No source found for the cost of a full SGS or TÜV factory audit commissioned by the buyer (as opposed to a pre-shipment inspection).

## Q2. Testing: which tests matter, cost per test, turnaround, labs in the UAE versus India and Asia, sample sizes

### Takeaway
Every test in the 50-wash standard is a routine, cheap test at Asian labs, typically USD 4–20 per test per fabric or colour. Indian lab list prices run about INR 250–1,650 per test. The expensive part is the extended washing: 50 ISO 6330 cycles plus pilling and colour checks at intervals is a custom programme. One Bangladesh lab's price of USD 4 per extra wash cycle suggests a 50-cycle programme of about USD 200 per fabric or colour at Asian rates; expect several times more at SGS, Intertek or BV in Dubai. Dubai Central Laboratory (government) publishes UAE-based colourfastness prices of AED 30–200 with 2–5 working-day turnaround.

### Inferences
**Recommended test panel for the 50-wash standard** (method names as in the brief; prices from the ATIRA and GMS lists above):
1. Dimensional change: ISO 6330 wash + ISO 5077 measurement, at 1, 5, 15 and 50 cycles. Pass if ≤3%.
2. Spirality (knit tee): ISO 16322-2.
3. Pilling: ISO 12945-2 Martindale on fabric, or ISO 12945-1 box. Also re-grade pilling on the washed specimens after 15 and 50 washes.
4. Abrasion: ISO 12947-2/-3, 20–30k rubs for the shorts.
5. Colour fastness: ISO 105-C06 (wash), E04 (perspiration), X12 (rubbing), B02 (light).
6. Stretch and recovery for the shorts: ISO 20932, ASTM D3107 for wovens, or EN 14704-1 as GMS lists it.
7. Moisture management for the tee: AATCC 197/198 wicking; AATCC 195 if the lab has the equipment.
8. UPF (AS/NZS 4399) only if you will make a UPF claim.

**Estimated budget:**
- At Asian labs: about USD 120–250 per fabric or colour for the core panel (rough sum of GMS prices plus a 50-cycle extension at USD 4 per cycle ≈ USD 200).
- At ATIRA-type Indian rates: about INR 5,000–10,000, plus 18% GST, plus "variable" items.
- At SGS, Intertek or BV Dubai: plausibly 2–5× higher. **These figures are estimates; get written quotes.**
- Test the darkest shade (the colour-fastness worst case) and the lightest shade (pilling visibility) at a minimum. Re-test each bulk lot for shrinkage and C06.

**Sample size:**
- No source found. Practitioner rule of thumb (unverified): about 1–2 m at full width per fabric or colour for a full physical and colourfastness panel. Extended washing and garment-level tests may need finished garments.
- For a 50-wash programme, send at least 2–3 m, or 3–5 sewn garments per colour. Confirm with the lab.

**Turnaround:**
- Only DCL published one (2–5 working days for colourfastness).
- A 50-wash programme will take weeks, given about 1 hour or more per cycle plus drying. I found no published turnaround for multi-cycle programmes.

### Gaps
- No published 2025–26 price lists found for SGS Dubai, Intertek Dubai, BV UAE, TÜV UAE, or China labs (SGS/Intertek/BV Shanghai, Guangzhou, Ningbo). These must be quoted directly. Chinese third-party labs are often cheaper than Western-branded ones, but I have no source for that.
- Not verified in this session (standard names taken from the brief only): AATCC 195/198, AATCC 199/201 drying rate, ISO 20932, ASTM D2594/D3107, AS/NZS 4399, and the exact current edition years.
- ATIRA lists pilling and abrasion as "Variable", so no INR figure is available for them.
- No sourced minimum sample sizes per test.

## Q3. Pre-shipment and fabric inspection services and costs

### Takeaway
Third-party inspection from QIMA, SGS, BV, Intertek or V-Trust in China costs about USD 250–400 per man-day, all-in. For a 300–1,000 m fabric order, one man-day is usually enough. Make the balance payment conditional on a passed report.

### Cited Findings
- A standard pre-shipment inspection costs about USD 250–400 per man-day from firms such as QIMA, SGS, Bureau Veritas or Intertek. Large or complex lots need more than one man-day. — [ChinaMakersHub](https://chinamakershub.com/journal/product-inspection-china-before-shipment)
- Alibaba's own seller blog says Southeast Asian regional inspections typically run USD 250–350 per man-day. — [Alibaba seller blog: factory inspection](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/industrial-services/factory-inspection-guide-alibaba-b2b)
- QIMA advertises "all-inclusive pricing for inspections", same-day reports and coverage in 100+ countries. Its "Guided Inspections" are remote: a QIMA supervisor works with factory staff within 48 hours. — [QIMA product inspections](https://www.qima.com/consumer-products/product-inspections); [QIMA guided/remote](https://www.qima.com/consumer-products/product-inspections/remote-inspection)
- A third-party inspection report is the evidence Alibaba expects for a Trade Assurance quality claim. — [Alibaba seller blog 2026](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/b2b-trade/trade-assurance-complete-guide-alibaba-secure-payment)

### Inferences
- For fabric rolls, ask for a fabric inspection to the 4-point system: typically 10% of rolls or yardage, with a points-per-100-square-yards limit (for example ≤20–28). Also ask the inspector to:
  - check width and GSM against the PO;
  - compare shade against the approved lab dip, including shade banding between rolls (side-centre-side and roll-to-roll);
  - check bow and skew;
  - draw lab samples from bulk to courier for C06 and shrinkage testing.

  This combines inspection and testing in one visit. The 4-point specifics are standard industry practice but I did not source them in this session.
- At this order size, an inspection fee of USD 300–400 may be 5–15% of the fabric value. It is still cheaper than one wasted cut in Ajman.

### Gaps
- No V-Trust or SGS price pages were retrieved. No India-specific per-man-day figures were found (likely similar or lower; unverified).
- No sourced 4-point system thresholds.

## Q4. Landed cost to the UAE: consumption, freight, duty, VAT, CEPA

### Takeaway
Landed cost ≈ fabric FOB + freight + insurance (gives CIF) + 5% UAE customs duty on CIF + 5% import VAT on (CIF + duty), recoverable if VAT-registered + clearance and handling + inland haulage to Ajman.
- Indian-origin knit fabric with a valid CEPA certificate of origin can enter at 0% duty if its tariff line is in the UAE's elimination schedule. Chapter 60 knit fabric must meet the rule "CTH + 40% value added".
- For 300–1,000 garments the fabric weighs only about 50–250 kg per style, so air freight (about USD 3–6/kg from China) is competitive with LCL once LCL minimums and destination charges are counted.

### Cited Findings
**Duty and VAT**
- The UAE generally charges 5% customs duty on CIF value. Import VAT of 5% is charged on customs value plus duty. Worked example: CIF AED 10,000 → duty AED 500 → VAT 5% × 10,500 = AED 525. — [Traddal](https://traddal.com/resources/calculate-duties-taxes-imports-united-arab-emirates); [Middle East Briefing](https://www.middleeastbriefing.com/doing-business-guide/uae/taxation-and-accounting/custom-duties-export-import-tax-uae); [Global Trade Plaza](https://globaltradeplaza.com/blog/vat-on-customs-duty-in-the-uae-documents-and-payment-process)
- Import VAT is 5% on customs value plus duty and can be claimed as input tax. This is a LinkedIn post from an accountant, so it is a secondary source. — [LinkedIn, M. Qasim FCCA](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/muhammadqasimfcca_import-vat-in-the-uae-2026-customs-declarations-activity-7518159949437231105-Dbsg)

**UAE–India CEPA**
- In force since 1 May 2022. It removes tariffs on more than 80% of products, with some products at zero tariff. — [UAE Ministry of Economy (MoET)](https://www.moet.gov.ae/en/cepa_india)
- Duties fall to zero on over 80% of tariff lines immediately and on 97% over time. — [TradeLane blog](https://blog.tradewin.net/india-uae-cepa-impact-and-opportunities)
- The UAE eliminates duties on Indian goods according to Annex 2B (its schedule of specific tariff commitments). — [CEPA text, MoET](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/1347101/Final+Agreement_UAE+India+CEPA.pdf)
- Product-specific rule for Chapter 60 (knitted or crocheted fabrics): **CTH + VA 40%** (change of tariff heading plus 40% value added).
- Certificate of origin rules: it must carry a unique serial number and be issued at, before, or within 5 working days of export. Retrospective issue is possible in exceptional cases.
- Indian sources describe CEPA as removing the UAE's 5% duty on a large share of Indian goods (for example jewellery), worth about USD 26 bn of products. — [PIB India](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1799756); [Indian Trade Portal](https://indiantradeportal.in/vs.jsp?lang=0&id=0,959,10581,28177,28189)

### Inferences
**All figures in this subsection are ESTIMATES. The consumption geometry is my own and unsourced; confirm it with the Ajman factory's marker.**

**Consumption**
- T-shirt (size M, short sleeve, 160–180 cm open-width knit): about 0.75–1.0 m per garment including about 10–15% cutting waste. Tubular or narrower fabric pushes this up.
- Shorts (stretch woven, 140–150 cm width): about 0.6–0.9 m per pair, depending on length, pockets and waistband. Add 3–5% for shrinkage and end loss, plus extra for testing and samples.

**Weight per garment**
- Tee: 0.9 m × 1.7 m × 140 g/m² ≈ 0.21 kg of fabric. 1,000 tees ≈ 210 kg (about 900 m).
- Shorts: 0.75 m × 1.45 m × 130 g/m² ≈ 0.14 kg. 1,000 pairs ≈ 140 kg (about 750 m).
- At 300 units per style, both styles together are only about 105 kg.

**Freight per garment**
- Air at USD 3.5–6/kg on 210 kg ≈ USD 735–1,260, about USD 0.7–1.3 per tee. Add airline, handling and destination charges.
- LCL: about 1–1.5 CBM (knit rolls very roughly 200–300 kg/CBM; unverified) at USD 65–150/CBM is only about USD 100–225 of ocean freight. However, origin and destination LCL charges (CFS, DO, documentation, clearance) and trucking Jebel Ali → Ajman are fixed costs I could not source. They can easily exceed the ocean freight. Total LCL door cost may land in the same range as air for loads under about 300 kg. Get door-to-door quotes for both modes.
- Transit: sea is about 15–22 days port-to-port plus consolidation and clearance; air is 1–3 days.

- That is about USD 6–6.7 per tee for fabric landed in Ajman, or about USD 6.7–7.4/m.
- Fixed costs (inspection, testing, clearance) weigh heavily at 300 units: they roughly double per unit.

**CEPA**
- For Indian knit fabric, ask the mill to issue a CEPA certificate of origin. The rule CTH + 40% value added is typically met when the fabric is knitted (and dyed) in India from yarn.
- The 5% duty saving on CIF is small in absolute terms at this order size (about USD 150–250 per order), but it is free.
- Check the specific HS line (6006.xx for weft knits; 5407/5512/5515-type headings for synthetic stretch wovens) against Annex 2B.

**Swatches**
- Courier costs for swatches and lab dips (DHL, FedEx or Aramex, about 0.5–2 kg) from China or India to the UAE were not sourced. Budget them as a line item and treat any figure as an estimate.

### Gaps
- Did not confirm from the UAE Federal Customs Authority or GCC Unified Tariff that specific HS lines for synthetic knit fabric (Ch. 60) and synthetic woven fabric (Ch. 54/55) are 5% rather than a different rate. Secondary sources say 5% for "most goods".
- Did not confirm whether Chapter 60 and the relevant woven lines are in the UAE's immediate-elimination category under CEPA Annex 2B, or are staged or excluded.

## Q5. What to ask suppliers in an RFQ

### Takeaway
The RFQ should turn the 50-wash standard into measurable, contract-ready specs and pass/fail methods. It should require recent test reports issued to that mill for that article. It should also force disclosure of whether the supplier knits, weaves and dyes in-house, because that determines who fixes a failure.

### Cited Findings
- Test reports should be recent (12–18 months), issued to the specific factory, and cover the exact specification. Verify them with the issuing lab. — [Aqualora](https://www.aqualora.us/blog/vet-alibaba-suppliers-red-flags)
- Ask for the business licence (营业执照) and verify the registration number on gsxt.gov.cn. A genuine supplier provides it within about 24 hours. — [Aqualora](https://www.aqualora.us/blog/vet-alibaba-suppliers-red-flags); [ChineseCheck](https://chinesecheck.com/blog/identify-real-factory-vs-trading-company)
- Trade Assurance quality claims need objective standards written into the order. — [Alibaba seller blog 2026](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/b2b-trade/trade-assurance-complete-guide-alibaba-secure-payment)

### Inferences
Suggested RFQ checklist (synthesised; not from a single source):

1. **Identity**
   - Legal name (Chinese or Indian registered name).
   - Unified Social Credit Code (China) or GSTIN/IEC (India).
   - Whether the supplier knits or weaves, dyes and finishes in-house or outsources.
   - Mill name if a trader or converter.
   - Factory address. Willingness to do a live video walk-through.
2. **Fabric specification**
   - Article number.
   - Composition (%) and yarn: for example 88/12 polyester/elastane or nylon/elastane; recycled content and its proof.
   - Construction: jersey, interlock, piqué; plain or twill weave, 2-way or 4-way stretch.
   - GSM ± tolerance. Cuttable width (open or tubular). Finish (wicking, anti-odour, DWR, peach).
3. **Performance data and test reports**
   - Dated, issued by an accredited lab, for this article.
   - Covering: ISO 6330/5077 shrinkage, ISO 16322 spirality, ISO 12945 pilling, ISO 12947 abrasion, ISO 105 C06/E04/X12/B02, stretch and recovery (ISO 20932 or ASTM D2594/D3107), and AATCC 195/197/198 wicking.
   - Whether the supplier will guarantee these values on bulk, and re-test each lot.
4. **Commercial**
   - MOQ per colour and per article. Surcharge below MOQ.
   - Greige or stock-service availability.
   - Price per metre and per kg at 300 / 600 / 1,000 m.
   - Incoterm (FOB or CIF Jebel Ali).
   - Sample and swatch charges.
5. **Colour**
   - Colour card or stock colours.
   - Lab dip cost and lead time (number of submits).
   - Pantone or TCX reference. Light source (D65/TL84). Tolerance (ΔE).
   - Shade banding and roll-to-roll continuity policy.
6. **Lead times**
   - Sampling or hand-loom.
   - Lab dip.
   - Bulk production after lab-dip approval and deposit.
   - Shipping.
7. **Certifications**
   - OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certificate number (verifiable online).
   - bluesign, GRS (for recycled claims).
   - Factory audits (BSCI/amfori, SEDEX/SMETA, WRAP).
8. **Packing and QC**
   - Roll length and weight. Packing list per roll with shade lot.
   - Inspection standard (4-point), allowed points, and the consequence of failure.
   - Acceptance of third-party pre-shipment inspection and lab sampling from bulk.
9. **Payment**
   - Deposit percentage. Balance trigger: B/L copy or a passed inspection.
   - Trade Assurance availability. Claim and remedy terms for out-of-spec fabric.
10. **Documents**
    - Commercial invoice, packing list, B/L or AWB.
    - Certificate of origin: a CEPA COO for India; a standard COO for China.
    - HS code the supplier will declare.

### Gaps
- No single authoritative fabric-RFQ template was retrieved. The checklist is synthesised from verification and testing sources plus industry practice.
- Typical MOQs per colour for performance knits and stretch wovens from Chinese or Indian mills (often quoted at hundreds to over 1,000 m or kg per colour), and typical bulk lead times, were not sourced in this session.
