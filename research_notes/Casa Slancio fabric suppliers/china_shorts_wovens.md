## Q1. Fabric options and trade-offs (T400, "T800", nylon/spandex vs polyester/spandex, mechanical stretch) and what best approximates Toray Primeflex

### Takeaway
Primeflex is a spandex-free bicomponent ("spiral") yarn fabric. Toray offers it mainly in nylon, plus PTT/PET versions. The closest affordable Chinese equivalents are spandex-free PTT/PET bicomponent polyesters (LYCRA T400, or the generic Chinese "T800"). These give moderate stretch (about 25–45%) with good recovery and no rubber ageing. 90/10 nylon-spandex 4-way wovens at 130–150 gsm give more stretch and a softer, matte "premium" hand, but they rely on elastane, which degrades over time. The supplier market uses "T400" and "T800" loosely, so composition and test reports must be verified.

### Cited Findings
**Toray Primeflex (benchmark)**
- Primeflex is "a new feel stretch nylon textile" using "bicomponent structural nylon fiber that bonds two nylon polymers having different performance characteristics into a single thread." — [Toray Sports Fabrics](https://www.sportstextiles.toray/en/primeflex/index.html)
- The same Toray page also lists PTT/PET composite structure variants, "partially plant-based PTT", partially plant-based nylon polymer and recycled PET. It names applications as running/fitness, cycling, outdoor, golf, fishing and commuting, and garment types including bottoms. It claims quick-dry, "lightweight construction", and that "high durability withstands alkali processing and chemical washing." No stretch %, gsm or wash-cycle data is published there. — [Toray Sports Fabrics](https://www.sportstextiles.toray/en/primeflex/index.html)
- Primeflex "utiliz[es] component spiral yarn that allows the fabric to stretch and recover without using…" (snippet truncated). — [primeflex.toray](https://www.primeflex.toray/)
- A brand user describes it as "patented spiral yarn technology that allows more stretch and recovery without using heavy, water-absorbing elastics." — [Walter Sky (brand page)](https://waltersky.com/pages/fabrics-technical-specs)

**LYCRA T400 (branded PTT/PET bicomponent)**
- LYCRA® T400® is sold by **The LYCRA Company**. It is "an inherently elastic elastomultiester (or elasterell-p) polyester fiber" that "can replace textured yarn in just about any stretch textile". Its "inherent helical coils deliver comfort stretch and superior recovery", with "no spandex shine", and its polyester components "handle harsh denim processing and workwear laundering". It can be combined with LYCRA® (spandex) for more stretch. A T400 A air-jet-textured version for wovens gives a "cottony hand". — [The LYCRA Company – T400](https://www.lycra.com/en/business/search-technologies/lycra-t400-fiber)
- Lycra's pitch to mills: use it to "upgrade from standard textured yarns to achieve greater and more durable stretch and recovery", either alone (moderate stretch) or with LYCRA fibre (high stretch). — [one.lycra.com](https://one.lycra.com/en/business/search-technologies/lycra-t400-fiber)
- T400 EcoMade: "Enjoy up to 30% stretch". Lycra's own recycled/renewable content figures vary by page: "at least 50% recycled PET and 15% plant-based" ([lycra.com](https://www.lycra.com/en/business/search-technologies/lycra-t400-fiber)), "68% sustainable resources" ([thelycracompany.com](https://www.thelycracompany.com/en/business/search-technologies/lycrar-t400r-ecomade-fiber)), and "more than 65%" ([lycra.com news](https://www.lycra.com/en/business/news/why-choose-lycrar-t400r-ecomade-technology)). These figures are inconsistent across Lycra's own pages.
- T400 was "originally a DuPont/Invista technology". — [Yaakan Chemical Fiber (yarn seller)](https://www.yaakan.com/stretch-yarn-ptt-pbt-t400-t800-guide/)

**Generic "T800" / elastic polyester (Chinese market term)**
- Yarn seller Yaakan describes T400 and T800 as bicomponent yarns that combine two polyesters that shrink differently, with T800 as a "finer, softer" development. It claims stretch of T400 25–35% and T800 35–45%, versus spandex at 400–700%. It says polyester stretch yarns have "no rubber ageing", good chlorine/heat resistance and single-bath dyeing, while spandex "degrades over time". — [Yaakan, Jun 2026](https://www.yaakan.com/stretch-yarn-ptt-pbt-t400-t800-guide/)
- A Suzhou trader (Suzhou Redcolor International Trading) claims both are PTT/PET side-by-side, with T800 having "modifications to the polymer chains". Its claimed figures:
  - Recovery: T400 ~85%, T800 ≥92%.
  - Permanent deformation after 50,000 stretch cycles: T400 8–12%, T800 <3%.
  - Breaking strength: T400 ~10 cN/dtex, T800 ~18 cN/dtex.
  - Price: T800 greige can be ~4–5 RMB/m *cheaper* than T400.
  — [Redcolortex](https://www.redcolortex.com/news/industry-news/t800-vs-t400-stretch-fabric-what-makes-t800-different.html)
- **Conflict:** the two sources disagree on hand feel. Yaakan says T400 is "crisp" and T800 "soft, premium". Redcolor says T400 is "smoother and more silk-like" and T800 "more robust… crisp". Both are seller content with no test standard cited. — [Yaakan](https://www.yaakan.com/stretch-yarn-ptt-pbt-t400-t800-guide/); [Redcolortex](https://www.redcolortex.com/news/industry-news/t800-vs-t400-stretch-fabric-what-makes-t800-different.html)
- Mifu (a fabric seller) says T400 and T800 are "100% polyester bicomponent weaves that stretch without any elastane". — [Mifu](https://mifufabric.com/t400-fabric-manufacturer/)

**Labels on Alibaba are loose (red flag)**
- Suzhou Xingmai's "100% Polyester T400 weft stretch" listing has the specs 50D×50D, 68×50, 80 gsm, "Pongee". — [Alibaba specs](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Ready-to-Ship-100-Polyester-T400_1601157856480.html)

### Inferences
- **Closest Primeflex analogue:** Primeflex's core is self-crimping bicomponent filament with no elastane. The like-for-like Chinese option is therefore a PTT/PET bicomponent woven: genuine LYCRA T400, or a mill's "T800"/PTT-PET yarn. This is especially close if Casa Slancio picks the polyester route, which matches Primeflex's own PTT/PET variant.
- I found **no Chinese supplier openly offering a nylon/nylon bicomponent stretch woven**, which would be the true nylon-Primeflex analogue. Nylon "Primeflex-like" handle is therefore most likely to come from 90/10 nylon-spandex with a full-dull yarn.
- **Recommended shortlisting logic:**
  - (a) 100–125 gsm PTT/PET ("T400/T800") plain or ripstop with DWR, for the quick-dry, no-elastane, 50+ wash-durable story.
  - (b) 120–145 gsm 90/10 or 92/8 nylon-spandex full-dull 4-way, for maximum stretch and soft matte hand.
  - Insist on wash-cycle stretch/recovery tests either way.
- **Durability:** elastane-free PTT/PET should hold recovery better through repeated hot washing than spandex blends, based on Lycra's "laundering" claim and Yaakan's "no rubber ageing". However, no supplier published 50-wash recovery data. Casa Slancio should require lab reports: recovery after, e.g., 20/50 domestic washes, pilling and colour fastness. Exact test standards are the writer's choice.
- Polyester typically dries faster and resists UV yellowing better than nylon. Nylon gives the softer, "cooler", more premium matte hand. This is general textile knowledge, not sourced in this research.

### Gaps
- No primary Toray datasheet with Primeflex stretch %, gsm, or wash-durability figures was found (the Toray pages are qualitative).
- No independent (non-seller) test data compares T400 vs T800 vs spandex after 50 washes. The T800 figures above come from sellers and contradict each other on hand feel.
- "T800" has no identifiable trademark owner or standard definition in the sources found. Treat it as a generic trade name.
- The LYCRA Company's current corporate ownership was not checked in this research.

## Q2. Which Chinese weaving mills (Wujiang/Shengze, Suzhou, Changzhou, Zhejiang, Fujian, Guangdong) make these, and which list brand customers?

### Takeaway
The Shengze (Wujiang district, Suzhou) cluster dominates lightweight nylon- and polyester-spandex and T400/T800 wovens. Most low-MOQ Alibaba sellers there are trading companies or converters, not weavers. Mill-like companies with their own websites, plus bluesign system partners in Wujiang, are the better first-tier targets. Almost none publicly name brand customers.

### Inferences
- **Mill-vs-trader reality:** nearly every low-MOQ (1–150 m) Alibaba listing checked was a "Trading Company". The two "Manufacturer, Trading Company" profiles found (Wujiang Pengnuo, Changzhou Baixing) advertise MOQs of 500–1,000 m.
- **Practical route for 300–1,000 shorts:**
  - About 0.9–1.2 m per pair of shorts is a rough cutting assumption, not sourced. On that basis 300–1,000 shorts needs roughly 300–1,200 m in total, i.e. ~150–500 m per colour across 2–3 colours.
  - That is below most mills' custom-dye minimums. It fits either a trader/converter's stock greige dyed in small lots, or a mill's stock colours.
- **Most credible for a premium brand:** the bluesign-listed Wujiang mills (Flying, Jintang, Sunfeng, Shichen, etc.) and the Taiwanese-owned mainland plants (Concordia Jiangsu, Winsun) carry the strongest compliance signal. Expect higher MOQs and prices from them.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm which bluesign-listed Wujiang/Suzhou mills actually weave 110–145 gsm stretch wovens; product pages were not found or not scraped.
- Except Jintang (Walmart/IKEA), no mill publicly named sports or outdoor brand customers in the pages reviewed.
- No Changxing/Huzhou (Zhejiang) or Changle (Fujian) stretch-woven mill with verifiable details was found within the research budget.
- ISPO Textrends and Performance Days exhibitor lists were not reviewed.

### Inferences
- **Indicative advertised price bands (USD/m, ex-works, before shipping/duty):**
  - Nylon-spandex 4-way wovens, 130–150 gsm: ~$1.55–4.40, with full-dull premium-hand versions at the top of the range.
  - Polyester T400/T800-labelled wovens: ~$0.93–2.50.
  - All are far below Toray-tier pricing (not quantified here).
- **Suggested shortlist for sampling:**
  - Nylon-spandex: Meidao (spec and price fit), Haoming (full-dull, assessed, 100% on-time), Yaqixin (1 m MOQ), Wupro (free A4 swatch).
  - Spandex-free polyester: Canxing (T800 non-spandex), Pengnuo (assessed Manufacturer+Trading, T800).
  - Compliance-led longer-term partners: Wujiang Flying (bluesign) and Zhongtian.
  - Finishing partner: Jintang, for DWR and certified finishing.
- **Common red flags:**
  - Trading companies posing as factories (listing titles vs profile).
  - Impossible compositions ("100% polyester 5% spandex").
  - "T400/T800" labels on plain 50D pongee.
  - Stock-lot fabric with no reorder continuity.
  - Low on-time rates (Caveman 50%, Xingmai 85.7%, Meidao 86.4%).
  - Very new storefronts (Ronghao, 1 year).
- **Before bulk orders,** ask each supplier for:
  - real fibre composition and yarn source (e.g. genuine LYCRA T400 hangtag/certificate vs generic PTT/PET);
  - OEKO-TEX/bluesign/GRS certificate numbers, to check in the issuers' online databases;
  - stretch/recovery and pilling results after repeated washes;
  - per-colour MOQ for lab-dip/custom dyeing vs stock colours.

### Gaps
- Per-colour MOQs, sample policies (beyond Wupro's free A4 sample) and lead times were not published for most suppliers. Flying Textile's "3–12 months" is a seasonal range from its MIC page, not a quoted lead time.
- OEKO-TEX/GRS claims could not be checked against certificate databases. Only bluesign status was verified, against bluesign's own list.
- The lead details provided in the brief could not be confirmed: Laner's 94/6 120 gsm listing, Molun's T800 104 gsm listing, and Ronghao's T800 100 gsm MOQ 1,400 m listing. Alibaba search did not surface them.
- No mill in Changxing/Huzhou or Changle with verifiable stretch-woven details was found. Guangdong is represented only by a trader (Yaqixin).
- No supplier published independent wash-durability (50+ wash) or stretch-recovery test reports.
