# Indian (and Singaporean) suppliers of performance knit and stretch woven fabrics for Casa Slancio

Scope: performance T-shirt knit (120–160 gsm, soft, quick-dry, 50+ wash durability) and technical-shorts woven (110–145 gsm, 4-way or mechanical stretch, quick-dry). First runs are about 300–1,000 garments per style. Research date: 25 Nov 2026. All supplier prices, MOQs, capacities, client lists and "manufacturer" claims are SELF-REPORTED on supplier websites or B2B listings (IndiaMART, TradeIndia, Justdial, Instagram). None was independently audited. No supplier was contacted.

## Q1. Which Indian mills, vertical knitters and processors make premium performance knits and stretch wovens, and which can serve small brands?

### Takeaway
India has two tiers. (a) Large vertical groups (Arvind Advanced Materials, Shahi, Vardhman) do knits and wovens with performance claims. Their public pages give no MOQ, price or brand-specific performance-knit data, and they are built for big programmes. (b) Tirupur, Delhi/Ludhiana and Surat polyester sportswear-fabric specialists (Pranera, Paruthi Mills, Positex) publish polyester and poly-spandex knits in or near the 120–160 gsm range, and some publish "no MOQ" or ready-stock models. These fit 300–1,000-piece runs, but they are almost entirely polyester. I found no Indian supplier publicly offering a 120–160 gsm nylon/elastane or lyocell/modal performance jersey with stated certifications, so that remains a gap. For the shorts woven, the closest matches are Positex (mechanical-stretch and poly/nylon-spandex wovens) and Surat sellers listing 130–140 gsm mechanical-stretch and 130 gsm NS (nylon-spandex) lycra wovens.

### Inferences
- **Quick-reference shortlist for Casa Slancio.**
  - T-shirt knit, polyester route: Pranera (dry-fit about Rs 299/kg ≈ US$3.4–3.6/kg; poly-spandex interlock Rs 592/kg ≈ US$6.7–7/kg) and Paruthi Mills (130–160 gsm polyester qualities).
  - T-shirt knit, premium route: approach Arvind Advanced Materials for nylon/elastane or modal/lyocell jersey. Expect higher MOQs.
  - Shorts woven: Positex (mechanical-stretch, NS Lycra wovens; claims no MOQ on stock items), with Surat sellers as backups.
  - Use Ultron and Blue Pearl only to buy swatches or for stock-lot sampling.
- At about 0.15–0.2 kg of 140 gsm jersey per T-shirt, a 1,000-piece run needs roughly 150–250 kg. That is small for a mill but normal for Tirupur processors who dye per lot. Per-colour minimums of about 100–300 kg are plausible but were not verified.
- "No MOQ" offers from Pranera, Ultron and Positex almost certainly mean ready-stock colours and qualities only. Custom dyeing, finishing (wicking, anti-odour) or special blends will carry real lot minimums. Ask each supplier directly.
- The 080-4754xxxx phone format for Pranera looks like an IndiaMART call-routing number, not a direct factory line. Contact through the GST-verified IndiaMART storefront or LinkedIn and ask for a factory visit or video audit.
- None of the Tier B or C suppliers published OEKO-TEX, bluesign or Lenzing/Birla licence evidence, except Positex's GRS claim. For a "premium, 50+ wash" brand, ask for third-party test reports (AATCC 61 colourfastness to washing, pilling, wicking) and OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certificates before committing.

### Gaps
- No verified Indian source was found for a 120–160 gsm nylon/elastane, COOLMAX-licensed or Lenzing/Birla modal/lyocell performance jersey with stated MOQ and price. The Birla Cellulose and Lenzing partner lists were not reached.
- Raymond, Welspun, Siyaram, KPR Mill, Sintex, Grasim/Birla Cellulose and Reliance Recron performance lines were not verified in this session.
- No supplier published lead time. Sample policy is published only for Positex.
- Pranera's "170 gsm microfiber, MOQ 5 kg" details are unconfirmed.
- Exhibitor lists for Bharat Tex, Intertextile and Performance Days, and the Texprocil and AEPC directories, were not checked.

## Q2. UAE–India CEPA: duty on Indian-origin fabric into the UAE, and India→UAE shipping

### Takeaway
The CEPA has been in force since 1 May 2022. Under it, the UAE removed its 5% customs duty immediately on Indian textiles and apparel, covering 97% of UAE tariff lines and 99% of Indian exports by value. Indian-made performance fabric therefore enters the UAE at 0% customs duty, provided it meets CEPA rules of origin (substantial processing of up to 40% value addition for most products) and comes with a CEPA certificate of origin. India-made fabric that uses imported yarn (for example nylon from China) could still qualify, but the textile product-specific rules were not verified.

### Cited Findings
- The UAE is eliminating duty on over 97% of its tariff lines, which account for 99% of Indian exports to the UAE by value — [APCO "CEPA Decoded"](https://apcoworldwide.com/static/dc4cc3d1a3aed2944cd84f381fd66f74/INDIA-UAE-CEPA-DECODED_April-2022-1.pdf); [NSEZ CEPA FAQs](https://www.nsez.gov.in/Resources/Trade/FAQs%20on%20CEPA.pdf).
- "Immediate duty-free access covers all labour-intensive sectors such as … textiles and apparel …". Indian products that were "subject to 5% import duty by the UAE" got "immediate market access at zero duty", covering 90% of India's exports to the UAE by value. The deal took effect on 1 May 2022 — [APCO](https://apcoworldwide.com/static/dc4cc3d1a3aed2944cd84f381fd66f74/INDIA-UAE-CEPA-DECODED_April-2022-1.pdf).
- Rules of origin: "Stringent rules of origin criteria that reflect the requirement for substantial processing of up to 40% for most of the products" — [APCO](https://apcoworldwide.com/static/dc4cc3d1a3aed2944cd84f381fd66f74/INDIA-UAE-CEPA-DECODED_April-2022-1.pdf).
- The full agreement text, including the certificate of origin procedures, is on the UAE Ministry of Economy site — [MoE CEPA text](https://www.moet.gov.ae/documents/20121/1347101/Final+Agreement_UAE+India+CEPA.pdf).
- On the reverse direction, fabrics (Chapters 50–60) imported into India from the UAE are duty-free immediately — [Deloitte tax@hand](https://www.taxathand.com/article/24459/India/2022/India-and-UAE-sign-trade-agreement).
- Shipping: weekly LCL consolidation services from Tuticorin (the port nearest Tirupur) to Jebel Ali are advertised — [freight forwarder post](https://www.facebook.com/groups/freightforwardersworldwide/posts/24427607286906154/). LCL sea freight services into Jebel Ali also exist — [First Vocean](https://firstvocean.com/services/jebel-ali/lcl-sea-freight). These are low-quality sources and give no transit days or rates.

### Inferences
- The supplier must issue (or arrange) a CEPA certificate of origin. Without it, the standard 5% UAE duty applies. Brief suppliers on this upfront, since many small Tirupur traders may not have done CEPA paperwork before.
- Fabric made in India from Indian-spun polyester (for example Reliance) is the cleanest origin case. Nylon or elastane yarn imported from China may still pass the about-40% value-addition test, but check the product-specific rules for HS 6004/6006 (knits) and 5407 (synthetic filament wovens).

### Gaps
- The exact product-specific rules of origin for HS chapters 54, 60 and 61 under the CEPA were not extracted from Annex 3.
- UAE import VAT (5%, which in principle still applies at import and is recoverable for VAT-registered businesses) was not verified in this session.
- No reliable source was found for India→UAE port-to-port transit days or 2026 LCL or air-freight rates. Get quotes from forwarders. Rough industry expectation (unsourced): about 1 week by sea from Nhava Sheva or Mundra, a few days by air.

## Q3. Singapore: real fabric manufacturing, or only HQ and trading offices?

### Takeaway
I found no evidence of performance-fabric mills in Singapore. Every textile company that surfaced is an office-based trading, marketing or supply-chain firm:
- KCTEX International: 6 Shenton Way office, "textile supply chain solutions".
- Cosmostar Singapore: registered for "marketing & distribution" of technical textiles.
- Hunter Textile Singapore: a Chinatown Point office.

Singapore makes sense for a small UAE brand only as a contracting or sourcing intermediary for Taiwan, China or South-East Asian mills (one English-law counterparty, consolidated sourcing). It is not a production base, and it adds a margin layer compared with buying from Indian mills directly under CEPA.

### Cited Findings
- KCTEX International Ltd, 6 Shenton Way #24-09 OUE Downtown 2, Singapore 068809 — [Times Business Directory](https://www.timesbusinessdirectory.com/company-details/kctex-international-ltd/). It was incorporated 19 Jul 2006 and is classed under "Apparel & Textile Products" — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/profile/company/9266161Z:SP).
- KCTEX describes itself as "a world-renowned provider of textile supply chain solutions with an established presence in over 58 countries". It covers "Fiber, Yarn, Fabrics, Apparels to Home Textiles" and talks about "sourcing expertise". Its site names no mills — [KCTEX](https://kctexinternational.com/). Trade media calls it "a Singapore based global textile supply chain solution provider dealing in textile raw materials, yarns and fabrics" — [Filo](https://filo.it/en/textile-solutions-by-kctex/). Trade data shows KCTEX as an importer in Malaysia — [TradeMagellan](https://www.trademagellan.com/company-details/importer/kctex-international-ltd/8CD52A60E48C09F3D54B954CB91716AB).
- Cosmostar Singapore Pte Ltd, 3 Fraser Street #20-22 Duo Tower, Singapore. Its registered activity is "MKT & DIST TECHNICAL TEXTILE & RELATED PDTS FOR FOOTWEAR & APPAREL IND." — [SG company directory](https://sg.ltddir.com/companies/cosmostar-singapore-private-limited/).
- Open Supply Hub lists Hunter Textile Singapore Pte Ltd at 133 New Bridge Road #08-03, Chinatown Point, which is an office or retail tower address, not an industrial site — [Open Supply Hub](https://opensupplyhub.org/facilities/SG20210266KEJ22).
- Kompass's Singapore "fabrics for the textile industry" entries are described as "Exporters of all kinds of textile fabrics" — [Kompass](https://lb.kompass.com/z/sg/a/fabrics-for-the-textile-industry/1250124/).

### Inferences
- When Singapore entities appear as fabric suppliers, the fabric is almost certainly milled in Taiwan, China, Vietnam, Indonesia or Malaysia. The Singapore company adds margin, financing and coordination.
- Reasons a small UAE brand might still use one: access to Taiwanese or Japanese-grade nylon/elastane technical wovens that India does not readily offer at about 110–145 gsm, plus a single counterparty with Singapore-law contracts. These benefits come with no CEPA duty advantage, because the goods would not be of Indian origin. The UAE's 5% duty may apply unless another FTA covers the actual origin country.

### Gaps
- Singapore government statistics on textile manufacturing output (for example EDB or SingStat) were not checked. The "no mills" conclusion rests on the absence of evidence across directory and company results, not on an official dataset.
- KCTEX's actual partner mills and any India or Middle East office were not disclosed on its site.
